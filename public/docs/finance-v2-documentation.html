Finance V2 — Overview

What is this module and why it exists

Finance V2 is a complete double-entry bookkeeping system built into ASDairy. It provides professional accounting controls for the dairy business — tracking every rupee through structured accounts, fiscal periods, and audited journal entries.

What Finance V2 Provides

📋 Account Structure

Organize all financial accounts in a hierarchical tree — assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses.

📅 Period Control

Define monthly, quarterly, or yearly financial periods. Lock closed periods to protect historical data.

📝 Journal Entries

Record every transaction as balanced debit/credit entries with full audit trail.

📈 Financial Reports

Generate Trial Balance, General Ledger, Profit & Loss, and Balance Sheet from posted entries.

🔄 Auto-Posting

Expenses, salaries, and depreciation automatically post journal entries when marked as paid.

🔒 Immutable Records

Posted entries cannot be edited — only reversed. Complete audit trail maintained.

ℹ️
Finance V2 is accessible to all non-farmer roles. Farmers are excluded from the entire Finance V2 section by design.

Core Accounting Principle

// The Golden Rule of Double-Entry Bookkeeping Every transaction must have: → At least one DEBIT entry → At least one CREDIT entry → Total Debits MUST equal Total Credits // Normal Balance by Account Type Asset / Expense → Debit increases balance Liability / Equity / Revenue → Credit increases balance

Module 1 — Chart of Accounts

The backbone of your accounting system — defines every financial category

The Chart of Accounts (CoA) is a structured list of all financial accounts used in the dairy business. Every journal entry line must be assigned to an account from this list. Accounts can be arranged in a parent-child hierarchy for grouped reporting.

Revenue Money earned by the dairy Milk Sales, Calf Sales, Ghee/Butter Sales, Subsidy Income Credit

Features & Actions

  • Create, edit, and delete accounts via modal dialogs (no page reload)
  • Search accounts by code or name

Module 2 — Financial Periods

Define and control fiscal periods — monthly, quarterly, or yearly

Financial Periods define the time boundaries for accounting. Journal entries are assigned to a period. Closing a period locks the accounting records, preventing backdating and protecting historical financial data.

Period Types

📆 Month

Example: August 2026
Used for monthly P&L tracking and expense monitoring.

📆 Quarter

Example: Q2 FY2026-27
Groups three months for quarterly reporting.

📆 Year

Example: FY 2026-27
Full fiscal year — used for annual balance sheet and tax filing.

💡 Tip

You can run all three simultaneously — monthly for operations, quarterly for review, yearly for tax.

Period Status Lifecycle

Open
→
Closed
→
Locked
StatusWhat It MeansWho Can Change It
Open Journal entries can be created and posted for this period. Normal working state. Any authorised user
Closed Period has ended. Ledger balances are recalculated automatically. New entries are blocked. Any authorised user
Locked Period is permanently locked. No changes of any kind are allowed. Used for completed tax years. Cannot be unlocked

Close Period — What Happens

  • All posted journal entries in the period are summed per account
  • Ledger Balances table is calculated (opening + movement = closing)
  • Closing balance becomes the opening balance for the next period
  • Period status is changed to "Closed"
  • The "Close & Recalculate" button triggers all of this in one click
🔒
Once a period is set to Locked, it cannot be reopened or changed in any way. Only lock periods that are fully finalized (e.g. after tax filing).

Module 3 — Journal Entries

The core of double-entry bookkeeping — record every financial transaction

A Journal Entry is the formal record of a financial transaction. Every entry must have at least two lines (one debit, one credit) and must be perfectly balanced before it can be posted. Once posted, an entry is immutable — it can only be cancelled by creating a Reversal entry.

Entry Types

TypeWhen to UseExample
General Any transaction that does not fit other types Opening balance entries, adjustments
Sales Revenue-generating transactions Milk sale to cooperative, bulk sale invoice
Purchase Buying goods or services Feed purchase, medicine procurement
Payment Cash or bank payment made Paying a supplier invoice, electricity bill
Receipt Cash or bank money received Subsidy received, farmer payment received
Payroll Employee salary payments Monthly salary disbursement — auto-posted from Salary module
Depreciation Periodic asset value reduction Annual depreciation on machinery — auto-posted from Fixed Assets
Adjustment Year-end or period-end corrections Accruals, prepaid expense adjustments

Reversal Entry

When a posted entry contains an error, you cannot edit or delete it. Instead, create a Reversal Entry. The system automatically creates a new entry with all debits and credits swapped, effectively cancelling the original. Both entries remain in the ledger for full audit visibility.
  • Only posted entries can be reversed
  • Reversal entry is auto-created and immediately posted
  • Reversal entry reference is prefixed with REV-
  • Original entry status changes to "Reversed"
  • Both entries remain visible in the journal for audit trail
✅
Example: You posted a milk sale of ₹5,000 to the wrong account. Click Reverse → system creates REV-JE-2026-00023 with debits and credits swapped, cancelling the effect. Then create a correct new entry.

Module 4 — Financial Reports

Trial Balance, General Ledger, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet

1. Trial Balance

Lists every account with its total debits and total credits from all posted journal entries. The grand total of all debits must equal the grand total of all credits. A balanced trial balance confirms the books are mathematically correct.
ColumnDescription
Account CodeUnique account identifier
Account NameDescription of the account
TypeAsset / Liability / Equity / Revenue / Expense
Total DebitSum of all debit lines in posted entries
Total CreditSum of all credit lines in posted entries
  • Filter by Financial Period or view all-time
  • Green alert shown when balanced, red when not
  • Sorted by account code

2. General Ledger

Shows the complete transaction history for a single account — every journal entry line that touched that account, in chronological order, with a running balance.
ColumnDescription
DateJournal entry date
Entry #Clickable link to the full journal entry
DescriptionLine or entry narration
DebitAmount debited to this account
CreditAmount credited to this account
BalanceRunning balance after each transaction (Dr/Cr)
  • Filter by account, date from, date to
  • Shows closing balance at the bottom
  • Balance displayed as "Dr" or "Cr" based on normal balance side

3. Profit & Loss Statement

The P&L shows how much profit or loss the dairy made in a period. It summarizes Revenue accounts on one side and Expense accounts on the other. Net Income = Revenue − Expenses.

Revenue (Income) Side

All accounts of type Revenue. Net = Credit − Debit for each account.

Expense Side

All accounts of type Expense. Net = Debit − Credit for each account.

Net Income = Total Revenue − Total Expenses Positive = Profit 🟢 Negative = Loss 🔴
  • Filter by Financial Period or date range
  • Visual progress bar showing revenue vs expense ratio
  • Color-coded: Revenue in green, Expenses in red

4. Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet is a snapshot of the dairy's financial position at a point in time. It shows everything the business owns (Assets) versus everything it owes (Liabilities) and the owner's stake (Equity). Assets must always equal Liabilities + Equity.
Assets = Liabilities + Equity Left Side: Total Assets Right Side: Total Liabilities + Total Equity Both sides must be equal ✓
  • Filter by Financial Period
  • Balanced/Unbalanced indicator shown at top
  • Assets shown in blue, Liabilities in red, Equity in yellow
  • Combined right-side total shown for quick comparison

Module 5 — Auto-Posting Integration

Expenses, Salaries, and Depreciation automatically create journal entries

Finance V2 is integrated with three other modules. When you mark an expense as paid, save a salary as Paid, or record depreciation for a fixed asset — journal entries are created automatically in the background. No manual data entry needed.

1. Expense → Journal Entry

TriggerDebit EntryCredit Entry
Expense created or updated with status = Paid Expense Account (type: expense) Cash/Asset Account (type: asset)
ℹ️
Auto-posting only happens the first time an expense is marked as Paid. Changing it back and forth will not double-post.

2. Salary → Journal Entry

TriggerDebit EntryCredit Entry
Salary saved with status = Paid Salary Expense Account Cash/Asset Account
  • Reference: SAL-{salary_id}
  • Description: "Salary: {Employee Name} (Month Year)"
  • Entry type: Payroll
  • Only triggers when status changes from non-Paid to Paid

3. Fixed Asset Depreciation → Journal Entry

TriggerDebit EntryCredit Entry
Click "Post Depreciation" button on the asset page, select year Depreciation Expense Account Accumulated Depreciation Account (asset)
  • Reference: DEP-{asset_code}-{year}
  • Description: "Depreciation: {Asset Name} (FY {Year})"
  • Supports straight-line and reducing-balance methods
  • Entry type: Depreciation
⚠️
Auto-posting requires accounts to exist in the Chart of Accounts. You need at least one expense account and one asset account with Allow Direct Posting = Yes. For best results, create accounts named "Salary Expense", "Depreciation Expense", "Cash in Hand", and "Accumulated Depreciation".

ledger_balances Pre-computed account balances per period account_id, period_id, opening_balance, total_debit, total_credit, closing_balance

Key Constraints

  • Account codes are unique across the entire chart
  • Journal entries enforce: total_debit = total_credit
  • Ledger balances: unique per account + period combination
  • Journal lines cascade-delete when parent entry is deleted
  • Soft-delete not used — posted entries are never physically deleted
